~$ cat summary.txt

A "dual-role" assignment: one group builds an attack scenario, hiding data and erasing traces; another investigates the resulting image. In this phase, I investigated a forensic ESD-USB image (E01, FAT32, 32,768 MB) received from another group, in an insider-threat scenario: data exfiltration from an account with legitimate access, disguised as normal activity.

I followed NIST SP 800-86 and ISO/IEC 27037:2012 guidance, documenting each extraction with its corresponding hash. I identified three distinct concealment techniques, recovered two exfiltrated artifacts, and reconstructed the full incident narrative from a deleted log, without ever overstating what the evidence actually supports.

~$ cat findings.log

  1. 1. Swapped extension: a .png file with a ZIP/OOXML signature (50 4B 03 04): a spreadsheet with 20 accounts from the trhplus-demo.local domain, plaintext credentials
  2. 2. Polyglot (appended data): a valid JPEG hiding a full .xlsx starting at offset 0xBD72A; extracted with dd, revealing an annual budget with live formulas
  3. 3. Decoy scripts: Credential Stealer.bat and Data Stealer.bat, with threatening names but harmless content (just echo): a decoy to draw the investigator's attention away
  4. 4. Fake access control: a deleted Main.bat, recovered via strings from unallocated space, with the password "Secret123" in plaintext inside the code itself
  5. 5. Central evidence: a deleted exfiltration log, recovered with icat and decoded from base64, documenting an automated routine: scanning the Financeiro folder, compressing/obfuscating, transferring to the USB drive, and clearing Event Logs

I cross-validated the findings: foremost's carving recovered the same two artifacts already extracted by metadata/offset in Autopsy and on Kali, with matching SHA-256 hashes.

~$ cat limitations.txt

Confirmed: three concealment techniques, effective exfiltration of two OOXML artifacts, deliberate destruction of evidence (the log's CLEANUP phase), and cross-tool validation.

Not proven: the source is a data volume with no operating system, so there are no Windows logs to tie the action to a specific person; the "Disabled" status of the associated account comes from the exfiltrated table itself, not from the system. I did not name a direct culprit because the image does not support that claim; refusing to guess without evidence was a deliberate choice, not a limitation of the investigation.
