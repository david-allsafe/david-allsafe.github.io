~$ cat summary.txt

Complete enterprise Active Directory and network services infrastructure for the fictional organization Skynet, Lda., domain istec.pt, in a multi-site architecture (headquarters in Lisbon and three branches: Porto, Coimbra and Faro). Covers centralized authentication, standardized policies, high availability and disaster recovery.

Built entirely on a single laptop (ThinkPad T480, 32 GB), alternating between two mutually exclusive virtualization environments: Hyper-V (Labs 1–6) and VMware Workstation (Labs 7–8), both on the same isolated subnet 192.168.10.0/24.

Hub-and-spoke model: Lisbon HQ as the hub, Porto/Coimbra/Faro as spokes, mapped to 4 AD Sites over a single physical LAN. The branches were modeled as OUs (for differentiated GPO application and delegation by location); the real physical topology is single-site.

~$ cat challenges.txt

Case A: the error code pointed at the wrong place: Hyper-V blamed the SCSI controller, but the real cause was a deleted ISO folder. DC02, offline for over a day, came back with error 8524 (DNS); DNS was actually fine, these were residual counters, resolved with repadmin /syncall.

Case B: an "active" service isn't necessarily a working one: WinNAT reported Active: True and the internal network worked, but GW01's two adapters were on the same subnet, with no boundary left to translate. I started always validating with Test-NetConnection instead of trusting the service's own reported state.

Case C: the information was available and I just hadn't checked it yet: Windows Admin Center returned a connection reset. Network, route and firewall were all fine. The cause (port 443 instead of 6600) had been written in the event log from the start.

~$ cat conclusion.txt

Every critical service was confirmed by more than one command or console, never just by the state the service reported about itself. Every deviation from the lab guide (RRAS→WinNAT, hypervisor-level backup→agent-based) is documented and justified with a tested technical reason, not hidden.

The configurations implemented, including multi-site replication, DHCP failover, layered backups (3-2-1 rule), and VM replication/Live Migration, are the same ones used in production, where high availability and disaster recovery aren't optional.
